### Passage 1 (Q21–Q25)

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

> The lighthouse stood at the edge of Marang Bay, tall and white against the grey sky. For sixty years it had warned ships away from the jagged rocks that lay hidden beneath the waves. Old Mr. Halloran had kept the light burning every single night, climbing the two hundred and twelve steps to trim the wick and polish the great glass lens until it shone like a diamond.
>
> One stormy November evening, the electricity failed across the whole coast. The town below sank into darkness, and far out at sea a fishing boat lost its way in the crashing rain. Mr. Halloran, now old and slow, could not reach the top of the tower fast enough. His granddaughter, Nadia, who was visiting for the holidays, understood at once what had to be done. Without a word she took the oil lamp, wrapped her scarf tightly around her neck, and began to climb.
>
> The steps were cold and narrow, and the wind screamed through the cracks in the stone. Twice she nearly slipped, but she thought of the fishermen in the boat and kept going. At last she reached the top, lit the lamp behind the lens, and turned the mechanism so that the beam swept slowly across the sea. Minutes later, the little boat found its way safely into the harbour. The fishermen never learned the name of the girl who had saved them, but Nadia knew, and that was enough.

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** "Mischievous" is the correct spelling (no extra "i" before "-ous").
**Q2.** "Accommodate" takes double "c" and double "m".
**Q3.** We "conduct an experiment" — the standard collocation.
**Q4.** We "pay attention" — the fixed collocation.
**Q5.** "Called off" means cancelled.
**Q6.** "Give away" means to donate/hand over freely.
**Q7.** "Let the cat out of the bag" means to reveal a secret.
**Q8.** The proverb is "A stitch in time saves nine."
**Q9.** An "illiterate" person cannot read or write.
**Q10.** A "library" is a place where books are kept for reading.
**Q11.** "Abundant" means plentiful.
**Q12.** The antonym of "ancient" is "modern".
**Q13.** Past perfect "had left" pairs with the earlier past action.
**Q14.** "An honest" — the silent "h" gives a vowel sound, so "an".
**Q15.** Jumping "over" the wall shows motion across the top.
**Q16.** "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "was".
**Q17.** "Must" expresses obligation/law.
**Q18.** Positive statement takes a negative tag: "haven't they".
**Q19.** Question mark inside quotes and "Mother" capitalised as a name.
**Q20.** "Everyone" is singular, so "is".
**Q21.** The passage states it warned ships away from hidden rocks.
**Q22.** He was "old and slow" and could not climb fast enough.
**Q23.** The simile stresses how bright and clean the polished lens was.
**Q24.** Climbing despite danger to save others shows courage.
**Q25.** She needed no thanks; helping was reward enough.
**Q26.** The notice fixes the drive for Saturday, 16 August 2026.
**Q27.** Each House must plant a minimum of twenty saplings.
**Q28.** Students bring their own gloves and water bottle.
**Q29.** Names must be given to House Captains by 12 August 2026.
**Q30.** The House caring best for its saplings over a month wins.
**Q31.** Accepting an invitation politely: "I'd love to, thank you!"
**Q32.** Correct reply to praise: "Thank you very much, Ma'am."
**Q33.** A gracious host says "You are most welcome."
**Q34.** Polite way to ask for a repeat: "I beg your pardon..."
**Q35.** Accepting an apology: "That's quite all right."
**Q36.** A fitting reply to the doctor: "Much better, thank you."
**Q37.** Helpful directions: "Of course. Turn left at the second signal."
**Q38.** Sympathetic response: "Oh no, I'm so sorry to hear that."
**Q39.** Lending politely: "Certainly, here you are."
**Q40.** Polite request: "Could you please pass me the salt?"
**Q41.** Agreeing to rest: "That's a good idea; I'll rest for a while."
**Q42.** Offering the seat: "No, please go ahead and sit down."
**Q43.** Responsible reply: "I'll make sure to return it on time."
**Q44.** Correct congratulation: "Congratulations on your success!"
**Q45.** Assuring punctuality: "I'll be there on time, Coach."
**Q46.** With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "teacher" (singular), so it should be "was able to", not "were".
**Q47.** "Face the music" means to accept the unpleasant consequences.
**Q48.** "Hardly...when" is the correct correlative structure with inversion.
**Q49.** Folded arms, glancing at the door and leaving early signal discomfort and eagerness to go.
**Q50.** "Meticulous" (very careful about detail) fits option A correctly.
