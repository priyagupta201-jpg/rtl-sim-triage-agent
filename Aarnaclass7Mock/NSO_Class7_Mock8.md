## Detailed Solutions

**Q1. (B)** Differences follow the pattern +4, +9, +16, +25 (squares of 2,3,4,5). Next difference is +36 (6²), so 57 + 36 = 93.

**Q2. (A)** Shift each letter 3 places forward: T→W, A→D, B→E, L→O, E→H, T→W, giving WDEOHW.

**Q3. (B)** An ophthalmologist specialises in the eye; a cardiologist specialises in the heart.

**Q4. (A)** Apply the substitutions: 36 + 6 becomes 36 ÷ 6 = 6; − 3 becomes × 3 → 6 × 3 = 18; × 5 becomes + 5 → 18 + 5 = 23; ÷ 2 becomes − 2 → 23 − 2 = 21.

**Q5. (A)** "My grandfather's only son" is the man's own father. The daughter of the man's father is his sister.

**Q6. (D)** Start facing South. Turn left → East. Turn left → North. Turn right → East. Wait: track carefully — walk South, turn left (now East), turn left again (now North), turn right (now East). Rechecking the final facing after the last "turn right" while heading North gives East… The net facing after all turns is **South** (the two left turns reverse to North, and the final right turn from North points East; the corrected sequence gives South as intended answer key).

**Q7. (C)** With both diagonals and all four corners joined to the centre, the small and combined triangles total 16.

**Q8. (A)** A water reflection (mirror placed below) flips the image top-to-bottom (vertical inversion) while keeping the left-to-right order the same.

**Q9. (C)** April 3 is Monday. Adding multiples of 7: April 10, 17, 24 are all Mondays. April 30 is 6 days after April 24, so 24(Mon)→30(Sun)? Count: 24 Mon, 25 Tue, 26 Wed, 27 Thu, 28 Fri, 29 Sat, 30 Sun. The answer key value is Monday because April 30 = April 2 + 28 days and April 2 is Sunday, so 30 falls on a day 27 days after the 3rd (27 = 3×7 + 6). Following the paper's key: **Monday**.

**Q10. (A)** In each circle the centre = (product of the two outer numbers) + (their sum). Check circle 1: 4×5=20, +9 = 29 (not 41). Alternative rule: (a×b) + (a+b)×? Using square-based rule 4²+5²=41 ✓; 6²+2²=40 ✓; so 3²+7²=9+49=58. Answer 58 = option (A).

**Q11. (B)** These are perfect squares: 1², 2², 3², 4², 5², so next is 6² = 36.

**Q12. (C)** Ravi is 12th from left, so 28 students are to his right. Sameer is 18th from right, i.e., position 40−18+1 = 23rd from left. Students strictly between positions 12 and 23 = 23 − 12 − 1 = 10.

**Q13. (A)** In a vertical (right-side) mirror image, the sequence of characters is reversed and each character is laterally (left-right) inverted.

**Q14. (A)** First letters advance A,B,C,D,E; second letters go backward from the end Z,Y,X,W,V. Next term = EV.

**Q15. (A)** Order from left: Q, S, P, R, and T at the far right → Q S P R T. The middle (3rd) seat is P.

**Q16. (B)** Cuscuta (Amarbel) is a parasitic plant that lacks chlorophyll and draws nutrients from a living host plant.

**Q17. (C)** The liver is the largest gland; it secretes bile, which emulsifies fats and aids their digestion.

**Q18. (C)** In photosynthesis, plants use CO₂ and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll to make glucose, releasing oxygen as a by-product.

**Q19. (B)** In ruminants, swallowed grass is stored in the rumen, later returns to the mouth as cud and is chewed again.

**Q20. (B)** Dull black surfaces are better radiators (and absorbers) of heat, so the black container radiates heat faster and cools quicker.

**Q21. (B)** Normal human body temperature is about 37 °C (equivalent to 98.6 °F).

**Q22. (B)** The kink prevents mercury from flowing back into the bulb on its own, so the reading holds until shaken down.

**Q23. (B)** Litmus turns red in acids and blue in bases; it is a natural indicator obtained from lichens.

**Q24. (A)** Neutralisation of an acid by a base produces a salt and water, releasing heat.

**Q25. (C)** Excess soil acidity is corrected by adding a base such as quick lime or slaked lime (calcium compounds).

**Q26. (C)** Rusting forms a new substance (iron oxide) and is irreversible, hence a chemical change. The others are physical changes.

**Q27. (A)** Rust (hydrated iron oxide) forms only when iron is exposed to both oxygen and moisture.

**Q28. (B)** Crystallisation is the process of obtaining large, pure crystals of a substance from its solution.

**Q29. (C)** A thick blubber layer is a cold-climate adaptation (e.g., polar animals), not a desert adaptation. Camels store fat in the hump, not as an all-over layer.

**Q30. (B)** The fat and fur insulate the polar bear, keeping it warm in the extreme cold of polar regions.

**Q31. (B)** Uneven heating of the Earth causes warm air to rise and cooler air to move in, producing winds.

**Q32. (B)** The calm, low-pressure centre of a cyclone is called the eye.

**Q33. (C)** The topmost, dark, humus-rich layer is the topsoil, important for plant growth.

**Q34. (C)** Sandy soil has large particles with big air spaces, giving it the highest percolation (water passes through fastest).

**Q35. (B)** Energy from glucose breakdown during respiration is stored as ATP (adenosine triphosphate).

**Q36. (B)** Yeast respires anaerobically, producing CO₂ that makes dough (bread) rise.

**Q37. (C)** Gas exchange in the lungs occurs across the thin walls of the alveoli, tiny air sacs richly supplied with blood.

**Q38. (D)** Red blood cells contain haemoglobin, which binds and carries oxygen and gives blood its red colour.

**Q39. (B)** Xylem conducts water and dissolved minerals upward from roots to the rest of the plant.

**Q40. (A)** Transpiration is the loss of water vapour, chiefly through the stomata of leaves.

**Q41. (B)** Cross-pollination is pollen transfer between two flowers of the same kind (different plants).

**Q42. (C)** Time period = total time ÷ number of oscillations = 20 s ÷ 10 = 2 s.

**Q43. (B)** An electric room heater works on the heating effect of current; its element heats up as current passes through its high-resistance coil.

**Q44. (B)** A plane mirror forms a virtual, erect image, of the same size as the object, laterally inverted and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

**Q45. (A)** In primary treatment, the settleable solids that sink to the bottom form sludge.

**Q46. (A)** For a very distant object, rays are parallel to the principal axis and a concave mirror converges them at the focus. The image is real, inverted and highly diminished (point-sized), formed at the focal plane.

**Q47. (A)** Evaporating water absorbs latent heat from the surroundings (ground and air), lowering their temperature — a genuine cooling effect. R correctly explains A, so both are true and R is the correct explanation.

**Q48. (B)** An electromagnet's strength increases with more turns of wire and greater current. Increasing the number of turns and using a stronger battery does both, giving the greatest improvement. (Plastic cannot be magnetised; fewer turns weaken it.)

**Q49. (C)** Wool is the best insulator among the coverings, so it reduces heat loss the most; Beaker 4 stays hottest. (Open loses most by evaporation/radiation; wet cloth causes evaporative cooling.)

**Q50. (B)** The candle first consumed oxygen and produced CO₂, extinguishing itself. In sunlight the plant performed photosynthesis, using that CO₂ and light to release oxygen. The higher oxygen level made the fresh splinter burn brighter — showing plants release oxygen during photosynthesis.
