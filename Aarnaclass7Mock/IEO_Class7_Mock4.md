### Passage 1 (Q21–Q25)

Read the passage and answer the questions that follow.

> Deep in the highlands of Peru grows a plant that most people have never heard of, yet it may be one of the most useful crops in the world. It is called quinoa (pronounced "keen-wah"). For thousands of years, the people of the Andes mountains treated quinoa as a sacred grain. They called it the "mother grain" because it fed entire communities even when other crops failed.
>
> What makes quinoa so special is its remarkable ability to survive harsh conditions. It grows well in poor, dry soil where wheat and rice would wither. It can endure freezing nights and blazing days. Because of this toughness, farmers can grow it at altitudes higher than 3,500 metres, where few other food crops can live.
>
> Quinoa is also extraordinarily nutritious. Unlike most plant foods, it contains all the building blocks of protein that the human body needs. It is rich in fibre, vitamins and minerals, and it is naturally free of gluten. For these reasons, scientists studying how to feed a growing world population have taken a keen interest in this humble grain.
>
> Today quinoa is sold in supermarkets far from the Andes, and its popularity continues to spread. Yet the farmers who first cultivated it remind us of an important lesson: sometimes the most valuable treasures are the ones that have quietly nourished people for centuries.

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** (B) "Conscious" is the correct spelling.  
**Q2.** (A) "Millennium" has double 'l' and double 'n'.  
**Q3.** (B) A "groundbreaking" discovery is the natural collocation.  
**Q4.** (A) The fixed collocation is "pay attention".  
**Q5.** (B) "Called off" means cancelled.  
**Q6.** (A) "Turn off" the lights means switch them off.  
**Q7.** (B) "Face the music" means to accept unpleasant consequences.  
**Q8.** (C) Small savings adding up matches "Little drops of water make a mighty ocean."  
**Q9.** (B) A writer of dictionaries is a "lexicographer".  
**Q10.** (A) A place where money is coined is a "mint".  
**Q11.** (B) "Abundant" means plentiful.  
**Q12.** (C) The opposite of "genuine" is "fake".  
**Q13.** (B) Past perfect "had left" is used for the earlier past action.  
**Q14.** (B) "Honest" begins with a silent 'h' (vowel sound), so "an" is used.  
**Q15.** (A) One jumps "over" a wall.  
**Q16.** (B) "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "was".  
**Q17.** (C) Legal obligation is expressed with "must".  
**Q18.** (B) Positive statement with "has" takes the negative tag "hasn't she".  
**Q19.** (B) The question mark goes inside the quotes and "she asked" stays lowercase.  
**Q20.** (B) "Therefore" links good play with the resulting cheering.  
**Q21.** (B) The text says it fed communities even when other crops failed.  
**Q22.** (B) Quinoa endures cold, dry soil and harsh conditions.  
**Q23.** (A) It contains all the building blocks of protein the body needs.  
**Q24.** (B) "Wither" means to dry up and die.  
**Q25.** (B) The last paragraph states this directly.  
**Q26.** (B) The notice is open to all students aged 10 to 14.  
**Q27.** (B) A stamp is collected on the Reading Card for each finished book.  
**Q28.** (B) 12 books reaches the 10-book prize (tote bag) but not the 15-book prize.  
**Q29.** (C) Comic books do not count towards the challenge.  
**Q30.** (B) Registration is advised early because seats are limited.  
**Q31.** (A) A polite refusal with a reason is the appropriate reply to an invitation.  
**Q32.** (A) Ordering food is the fitting response to "ready to order?".  
**Q33.** (A) Accepting an apology graciously is appropriate.  
**Q34.** (A) Congratulating a friend on success is the correct response.  
**Q35.** (A) "Could you tell me where..." is the polite indirect question form.  
**Q36.** (A) "Could you" makes a polite request.  
**Q37.** (A) A relevant answer about the homework fits the question.  
**Q38.** (A) A warm, welcoming reply suits a thankful guest.  
**Q39.** (A) An apology with a valid reason answers "why were you late?".  
**Q40.** (A) "Shall I..." is used to offer help.  
**Q41.** (A) Paying and thanking is the appropriate response to a shopkeeper.  
**Q42.** (A) An enthusiastic description answers a question about a trip.  
**Q43.** (A) "May I..." asks for permission politely.  
**Q44.** (A) Thanking and agreeing to return the book on time is appropriate.  
**Q45.** (B) Polite disagreement acknowledges the other view before differing.  
**Q46.** (C) With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "assistants" (plural), so "was" should be "were".  
**Q47.** (B) In reported speech "will" becomes "would" and "tomorrow" becomes "the next day".  
**Q48.** (B) "A shot in the arm" means a sudden boost of encouragement or energy.  
**Q49.** (B) The warm coat, coffee and open laptop suggest he is still in the building working late.  
**Q50.** (C) Here "light" means cheerful/carefree, matching option (C).
