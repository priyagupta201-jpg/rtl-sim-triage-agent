# SOF IMO — International Mathematics Olympiad
## Class 7 — Mock Test 1

**Total Questions:** 50 | **Total Marks:** 60 | **Time:** 60 Minutes

**Instructions:**
1. There are 4 sections: Logical Reasoning (Q1–Q15), Mathematical Reasoning (Q16–Q35), Everyday Mathematics (Q36–Q45), and the Achievers Section (Q46–Q50).
2. Each question in Sections 1–3 carries **1 mark**. Each question in the Achievers Section carries **3 marks**.
3. Every question has exactly four options — only **one** is correct.
4. There is **no negative marking**.
5. Use of calculators is **not** permitted.

## Section 1: Logical Reasoning

**Q1.** Find the next number in the series: 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, ?

(A) Sister  (B) Cousin  (C) Aunt  (D) Niece

**Q4.** Choose the correct analogy: Day : Night :: Kind : ?
(A) Gentle  (B) Cruel  (C) Generous  (D) Helpful

**Q5.** A man walks 5 km towards the North, then turns right and walks 3 km, then turns right again and walks 5 km. In which direction is he now facing?

**Q6.** How many times does the digit 4 appear from 1 to 50?
(A) 13  (B) 14  (C) 15  (D) 16

(A) 14th  (B) 15th  (C) 16th  (D) 13th

(A) 3  (B) 5  (C) 9  (D) 11

**Q9.** If '+' means '×', '−' means '+', '×' means '÷', and '÷' means '−', then what is the value of: 8 + 2 − 12 × 4 ÷ 5?

**Q10.** Complete the series: AZ, BY, CX, DW, ?
(A) EV  (B) FV  (C) EU  (D) EW

**Q11.** Five friends are sitting in a row facing North. Aditi is to the left of Bhavya but to the right of Chirag. Deepak is to the right of Bhavya but to the left of Esha. Who is in the middle?

**Q12.** A cube is painted red on all faces and then cut into 27 identical smaller cubes. How many smaller cubes have exactly one face painted red?

**Q13.** If the day before yesterday was Thursday, what day will it be the day after tomorrow?

**Q14.** Choose the number pair that follows the same relationship as (7, 49):

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** Differences are 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 (odd numbers). 27 + 11 = **38**. → (B)

**Q2.** Each letter shifts +1: C→D, H→I, A→B, I→J, R→S. So TABLE → UBCMF. → (A)

**Q3.** "Grandfather's only son" = Rohan's father; his daughter = Rohan's **sister**. → (A)

**Q4.** Day and Night are opposites, so Kind's opposite is **Cruel**. → (B)

**Q5.** North, then right → East (3 km), then right → South (5 km). He faces **South**. → (B)

**Q6.** Digit 4 in units place: 4,14,24,34,44 (5 times); tens place: 40–49 (10 times). 5+10 = **15**. → (C)

**Q7.** Position from right = 25 − 11 + 1 = **15th**. → (B)

**Q8.** 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 are prime; **9 = 3×3** is not prime. → (C)

**Q9.** Substitute symbols (+→×, −→+, ×→÷, ÷→−): 8 × 2 + 12 ÷ 4 − 5 = 16 + 3 − 5 = **14**. → (C)

**Q10.** First letters A,B,C,D → next E. Second letters Z,Y,X,W → next V. So **EV**. → (A)

**Q11.** Order left→right: Chirag, Aditi, Bhavya, Deepak, Esha. Middle = **Aditi**. → (A)

**Q12.** In a 3×3×3 cube, the single centre cube of each face has exactly one painted face: 6 faces → **6** cubes. → (A)

**Q13.** Day before yesterday = Thursday → today = Saturday → day after tomorrow = **Monday**. → (B)

**Q14.** 49 = 7². Similarly 36 = 6². So **(6, 36)**. → (A)

**Q15.** Folding once and cutting the corner on the fold opens to **1** complete hole. → (A)

**Q16.** −15 − 8 + 12 = −23 + 12 = **−11**. → (A)

**Q17.** 5/7 − (−3/7) = 5/7 + 3/7 = **8/7**. → (B)

**Q18.** 0.6 × 0.05 = 0.030. → (B)

**Q19.** 3x = 16 + 5 = 21 → x = **7**. → (B)

**Q20.** Complementary → sum 90°. Parts 2+3=5; larger = 3/5 × 90 = **54°**. → (B)

**Q21.** 180 − (45 + 65) = 180 − 110 = **70°**. → (C)

**Q22.** 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = **72**. → (A)

**Q23.** 24/36 = (24÷12)/(36÷12) = **2/3**. → (A)

**Q24.** 15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = **36**. → (C)

**Q25.** 2(l + b) = 48 → l + b = 24 → b = 24 − 14 = **10 cm**. → (B)

**Q26.** (5a − 2a) + (3b + 4b) = **3a + 7b**. → (A)

**Q27.** Positives beat negatives; 3/4 > 1/2. Greatest = **3/4**. → (B)

**Q28.** Area = ½ × 12 × 5 = **30 cm²**. → (A)

**Q29.** Sum = 120; 120 ÷ 5 = **24**. → (C)

**Q30.** 5⁻² = 1/5² = **1/25**. → (B)

**Q31.** Odd count (5) of negative factors → product = **−1**. → (B)

**Q32.** Letter **H** has both a horizontal and vertical line of symmetry (two). → (B)

**Q33.** Every cuboid has **12 edges**. → (C)

**Q34.** 3/5 = x/20 → x = 3 × 4 = **12**. → (C)

**Q35.** −72 ÷ 9 = −8; 3 × (−2) = −6; −8 + (−6) = **−14**. → (A)

**Q36.** Profit = 300 − 250 = 50; % = 50/250 × 100 = **20%**. → (B)

**Q37.** 240 ÷ 4 = **60 km/h**. → (C)

**Q38.** SI = (5000 × 8 × 2)/100 = **₹800**. → (B)

**Q39.** 3½ − 1¾ = 7/2 − 7/4 = 14/4 − 7/4 = 7/4 = **1¾ kg**. → (A)

**Q40.** 25% wear glasses → 75% don't = 0.75 × 40 = **30**. → (C)

**Q41.** 450 ÷ 15 = **30 minutes**. → (B)

**Q42.** Sugar:flour = 2:5. Sugar 10 → multiplier 5 → flour = 5×5 = **25**. → (C)

**Q43.** Discount = 20% of 800 = 160; SP = 800 − 160 = **₹640**. → (B)

**Q44.** −4 + 9 = **5°C**. → (A)

**Q45.** 3 weeks = 21 days; 25 × 21 = **525 pages**. → (C)

**Q46.** Integers: 24, 26, 28 (sum = 78). Smallest × largest = 24 × 28 = **672**. → (B)

**Q47.** Exterior angle at R = angle P + angle Q → 110 = 65 + Q → Q = **45°**. → (B)

**Q48.** Let CP = 100. MP = 140. SP = 140 − 25% = 140 × 0.75 = 105. Profit = 5 → **5% profit**. → (A)

**Q49.** (i) 8+9 = 17 (R); (ii) 16 (P); (iii) 25−16 = 9 (Q); (iv) 27−8 = 19 (S). → **(B)**

**Q50.** 3/4 = 0.75 lies between 0.5 and 1, so A is true. R is a true general statement but does not specifically explain why 3/4 lies there. Both true, R not the correct explanation → **(B)**.
