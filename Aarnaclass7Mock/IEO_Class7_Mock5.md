### Passage 1 (Prose) — Read the passage and answer Q21–Q25.

> Deep in the highlands of Ethiopia, coffee began not as a drink but as a curious discovery. According to a popular legend, a goatherd named Kaldi noticed that his goats became unusually lively after nibbling the bright red berries of a certain shrub. Puzzled, Kaldi tried the berries himself and felt a burst of energy. He carried some to a nearby monastery, hoping the monks would share his excitement. The head monk, however, disapproved and threw the berries into a fire. Almost at once, a rich and pleasant aroma rose from the roasting beans. Curious, the monks raked the beans from the flames, ground them, and dissolved them in hot water — and so, according to the story, the first cup of coffee was brewed.
>
> Whether or not the legend is true, coffee soon spread from Africa to the Arabian Peninsula, where it was cultivated and traded on a large scale. By the fifteenth century, coffee houses had appeared in cities such as Mecca and Cairo. These were not merely places to drink; they became lively centres where people gathered to talk, listen to music, play games, and exchange news. In time, travellers carried coffee to Europe, where it was at first viewed with suspicion but later embraced with enthusiasm. Today, coffee is one of the most widely consumed beverages in the world, and few of those who sip it each morning pause to think of a wandering goatherd and his restless goats.

## Detailed Solutions

1. **A** — "Miscellaneous" is the correct spelling.
2. **B** — "Environment" is the correct spelling.
3. **B** — We "make a discovery"; the natural collocation here is *discovery*.
4. **A** — The fixed collocation is "take a breath."
5. **B** — "Put off" means to postpone.
6. **A** — "Turn off" the lights means to switch them off.
7. **A** — "Come clean" means to confess or tell the truth.
8. **B** — Timely action prevents larger trouble later.
9. **B** — A person who journeys to a sacred place is a *pilgrim*.
10. **C** — A *mint* is where money (coins) is made.
11. **A** — "Feeble" means weak.
12. **B** — The opposite of "spacious" is *cramped*.
13. **C** — Past perfect "had left" is used for the earlier of two past events.
14. **B** — "Honest" begins with a vowel sound, so "an" is used.
15. **A** — Jumping across the top of a wall takes "over."
16. **B** — "Neither of" takes a singular verb: *was*.
17. **C** — Legal obligation requires the modal "must."
18. **B** — Positive present-perfect statement takes negative tag "haven't they."
19. **B** — The question mark goes inside the quotation, followed by "she asked."
20. **B** — "Advice" is an uncountable noun and takes no article or plural.
21. **B** — Kaldi noticed his goats became unusually lively.
22. **B** — The text states the head monk "disapproved" of the berries.
23. **A** — A rich, pleasant aroma rose from the roasting beans.
24. **C** — Coffee houses became lively centres for talk, music and news.
25. **B** — "Viewed with suspicion" means Europeans distrusted coffee at first.
26. **B** — 1st to 15th August is fifteen days.
27. **B** — Only gently used, undamaged children's/teen books are accepted.
28. **C** — "For every three books donated" gives one entry.
29. **B** — Books go to rural schools that lack proper libraries.
30. **C** — Queries go to the librarian at the Help Desk.
31. **B** — A polite, logical declining of an invitation with a reason.
32. **B** — A helpful, relevant answer giving directions.
33. **A** — A gracious, reassuring response to an apology.
34. **B** — A polite, appropriate acceptance of praise.
35. **B** — A clear, relevant answer to the passenger's question.
36. **B** — "Could you..." is the polite request form.
37. **B** — A sincere apology fits an accidental action.
38. **A** — Directly and logically answers the mother's question.
39. **B** — A polite refusal of more food.
40. **A** — A gracious acknowledgement of congratulations.
41. **B** — "May I..." is used to ask permission politely.
42. **A** — A warm, courteous response to a friendly welcome.
43. **A** — A modest, appropriate reply to a compliment on the food.
44. **A** — Expresses enthusiastic agreement with the suggestion.
45. **A** — A calm, practical response to the librarian's news.
46. **B** — "Built on sand" means based on something weak and unreliable.
47. **C** — Subject "One" is singular, so "are" should be "is."
48. **B** — Both use "light" figuratively to mean knowledge/understanding.
49. **C** — Her calm, note-free delivery shows she knew the speech well.
50. **B** — Reported speech shifts "will" to "would," "tomorrow" to "the next day," "this" to "that."
