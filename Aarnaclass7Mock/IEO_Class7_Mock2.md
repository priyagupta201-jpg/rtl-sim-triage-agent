> The lighthouse stood at the edge of the rocky coast, its white tower rising against the grey sky. For nearly a hundred years, it had warned ships away from the dangerous reefs hidden beneath the waves. Old Mr. Fenwick had been its keeper for thirty of those years, climbing the narrow spiral staircase every evening to light the great lamp.
>
> The villagers rarely visited him, for the path to the lighthouse was steep and lonely. Yet Mr. Fenwick was never truly alone. Seabirds nested in the cliffs below, and every night the beam of his lamp swept across the ocean like a loyal friend keeping watch. He knew the moods of the sea better than anyone — when it was calm and silver, and when it turned black and furious.

Then, faint but steady, the light appeared, cutting through the mist. Following it carefully, they steered safely past the reefs and reached the harbour before dawn. The next morning, the grateful fishermen climbed the steep path to thank the old keeper, and for the first time in years, the lighthouse had visitors who stayed for tea.

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** "Occasion" — one 'c', one 's', double 's' spelling is a common error.  
**Q2.** "Millennium" has double 'l' and double 'n'.  
**Q3.** "Error in judgement" is the standard collocation for a serious mistake.  
**Q4.** We "make a decision"; "make" is the correct collocating verb.  
**Q5.** "Called off" means cancelled; the correct phrasal verb.  
**Q6.** "Pipe down" means to become quiet.  
**Q7.** "On cloud nine" means extremely happy.  
**Q8.** The proverb is "Haste makes waste."  
**Q9.** A "lexicographer" writes and edits dictionaries.  
**Q10.** An "aviary" is a place where birds are kept.  
**Q11.** "Fortitude" means courage or strength in facing difficulty.  
**Q12.** Antonym of "fragile" (easily broken) is "sturdy" (strong).  
**Q13.** Past perfect "had left" describes the earlier of two past actions.  
**Q14.** "An honest man" — 'h' is silent, so the vowel sound takes "an".  
**Q15.** "Jumped over the wall" indicates movement across the top of it.  
**Q16.** With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "teacher" (singular) → "was".  
**Q17.** "Must" expresses obligation or law.  
**Q18.** Positive statement with "has" takes the negative tag "hasn't she".  
**Q19.** The question mark stays inside the quotation and "asked" is lowercase — option B is correct.  
**Q20.** "Effect" is the noun meaning result/impact.  
**Q21.** The passage states he was keeper for thirty of those years.  
**Q22.** Villagers rarely visited because the path was steep and lonely.  
**Q23.** The beam of the lamp is compared to a loyal friend keeping watch.  
**Q24.** The steady light cutting through the fog guided them safely.  
**Q25.** Climbing the stairs every evening for years shows he was dedicated.  
**Q26.** The Literary Club is organising the competition.  
**Q27.** Names must be given to House Captains by 22 August 2026.  
**Q28.** The theme is "A Journey I Will Never Forget".  
**Q29.** The notice states costumes are not permitted.  
**Q30.** Contact the Literary Club Secretary in Room 14 for details.  
**Q31.** Reassuring the apologiser politely is the appropriate response.  
**Q32.** Politely declining and asking for the bill fits the context.  
**Q33.** Agreeing to help willingly is the courteous response.  
**Q34.** Accepting congratulations graciously is appropriate.  
**Q35.** Welcoming the guest warmly is the polite expression.  
**Q36.** A polite apology with a genuine reason is appropriate.  
**Q37.** Confirming and inviting the passenger aboard is correct.  
**Q38.** "Would you mind..." is a polite request form.  
**Q39.** "It's been ages! How have you been?" suits reunion with an old friend.  
**Q40.** Politely stating one's purpose is the natural customer response.  
**Q41.** Honest and cooperative reply is the appropriate one.  
**Q42.** "Let me give you a hand" is the idiom for offering help.  
**Q43.** A polite, positive self-description suits an interview.  
**Q44.** Encouraging a disappointed friend is the kind response.  
**Q45.** A cooperative reply respecting the closing time is appropriate.  
**Q46.** No error; "Hardly had...when" is the correct inversion structure.  
**Q47.** "Incriminating" evidence points to guilt — the precise word.  
**Q48.** "Grasp the nettle" means to tackle a difficult situation firmly.  
**Q49.** "Having worked for years, the scientist..." correctly uses a participial phrase.  
**Q50.** "Neither...is" — "neither" is singular and "acceptable to" is the correct usage.
