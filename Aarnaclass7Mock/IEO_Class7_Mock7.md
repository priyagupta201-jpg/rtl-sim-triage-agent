**Total Questions:** 50

### Instructions

1. There are **50 questions** divided into **four sections**.
2. Sections 1–3 contain questions of **1 mark** each (Q1–Q45). Section 4, the **Achievers Section**, contains questions of **3 marks** each (Q46–Q50).
3. Each question is a multiple-choice question with **exactly four options** — (A), (B), (C) and (D). Only **one** option is correct.
4. There is **no negative marking**.
5. Read every passage and question carefully before answering.
6. Use of dictionaries, calculators or electronic devices is **not permitted**.

### Passage 1 (Prose)

> The lighthouse at Cape Verity had stood for over a hundred years, its white tower rising above the jagged rocks like a silent guardian. For most of that time, a keeper had lived inside it, climbing the narrow spiral stairs every evening to light the great lamp so that ships could find their way home through the fog.
>
> Elara was the last of these keepers. Automated lights had replaced human hands almost everywhere else along the coast, but the machinery at Cape Verity was old and stubborn, and the shipping company had decided it was cheaper to keep one person on than to install new equipment. Elara did not mind. She had grown up in a noisy city, and the solitude of the lighthouse suited her the way a well-worn coat suits its owner.
>
> Her days followed a steady rhythm. She polished the brass fittings until they gleamed, recorded the weather in a leather-bound logbook, and repaired whatever the salt air had corroded. But her favourite task came at dusk, when she wound the mechanism that turned the lamp. The beam would sweep out across the dark water, and Elara liked to imagine the sailors far out at sea, glancing up and knowing exactly where they were.
>
> One autumn night a fierce storm rolled in. The wind screamed against the tower, and rain lashed the windows so hard that Elara feared the glass might shatter. Then, without warning, the lamp flickered and went dark. A gear had cracked. Somewhere out in that churning blackness, a fishing boat was making for the harbour, trusting a light that no longer shone.
>
> Elara did not hesitate. She hauled an old oil lantern from a cupboard, lit it, and carried it up to the very top of the tower. Bracing herself against the gallery rail, she held the small flame high and swung it in slow, deliberate arcs, hour after hour, until her arms ached and her fingers were numb. It was nothing like the mighty beam of the lamp, but it was steady, and it was human, and in the grey light of morning she saw the fishing boat safe at its mooring in the harbour below.

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** (B) "Conscientious" is the correct spelling.
**Q2.** (A) "Millennium" (double l, double n) is correct.
**Q3.** (B) A "groundbreaking discovery" is the standard collocation for something innovative.
**Q4.** (B) One "makes a decision," not does/takes/gives one.
**Q5.** (A) To "give in" means to surrender or yield.
**Q6.** (A) "Called off" means cancelled.
**Q7.** (B) "Open a can of worms" means to create a set of new, complicated problems.
**Q8.** (C) Saving small amounts leading to a large sum fits "Little drops of water make a mighty ocean."
**Q9.** (A) A "polyglot" speaks many languages; a linguist studies language.
**Q10.** (B) A "panacea" is a cure-all for every problem.
**Q11.** (A) "Tenacious" means persistent/determined.
**Q12.** (B) "Frugal" (economical) is opposite to "extravagant."
**Q13.** (C) "Conceal" (hide) is opposite to "reveal."
**Q14.** (B) Past perfect "had left" describes the earlier of two past actions.
**Q15.** (B) "Honest" begins with a silent 'h' (vowel sound), so "an" is correct.
**Q16.** (C) We use "in" with centuries.
**Q17.** (C) With "neither...nor," the verb agrees with the nearer subject "workers" (plural) → "are."
**Q18.** (A) "Mustn't" expresses strong prohibition due to danger.
**Q19.** (A) "Let's..." takes the tag "shall we."
**Q20.** (B) The question mark goes inside the quotation marks; the reporting clause follows.
**Q21.** (B) The old machinery made keeping her cheaper than installing new equipment.
**Q22.** (B) The simile shows the solitude felt comfortable and familiar to her.
**Q23.** (C) "A gear had cracked," causing the lamp to go dark.
**Q24.** (C) She lit an oil lantern and swung it in slow arcs for hours.
**Q25.** (B) Holding the lantern for hours despite aching arms shows perseverance.
**Q26.** (B) Relocation is due to repair work on the ground-floor ceiling.
**Q27.** (C) Saturday story-time sessions are suspended until further notice.
**Q28.** (C) The lift is reserved for members who find the stairs difficult.
**Q29.** (B) The repair is expected to take six weeks.
**Q30.** (B) The notice is courteous and gives clear information — polite and informative.
**Q31.** (B) A reassuring reply suits a nervous friend.
**Q32.** (B) "Not at all" politely agrees to a "Would you mind..." request.
**Q33.** (B) "Are you ready to order?" fits the guest's reply of ordering food.
**Q34.** (B) "Well done, you deserved it!" congratulates appropriately.
**Q35.** (B) A polite, helpful answer giving correct directions.
**Q36.** (B) A gracious response accepting an apology.
**Q37.** (A) Giving clear directions answers the request for the way.
**Q38.** (B) "Sorry to interrupt, but may I add something?" is polite.
**Q39.** (A) A warm, grateful reply from a departing guest.
**Q40.** (B) A gracious thank-you accepting congratulations.
**Q41.** (A) A willing, professional acceptance of responsibility.
**Q42.** (B) Politely asking for a repeat due to a poor line.
**Q43.** (B) A relevant, polite update on one's health.
**Q44.** (B) Declining warmly while giving a reason is the polite form.
**Q45.** (B) A thoughtful, motivated reason impresses an interviewer.
**Q46.** (B) Humming and returning to a happy habit imply the letter brought comfort/good news after hard months.
**Q47.** (C) "Neither of..." takes a singular verb, so "were considered" should be "was considered."
**Q48.** (A) "Bury the hatchet" (make peace) is used correctly by former rivals reconciling.
**Q49.** (C) "Scathing" (harshly critical) fits a review so severe even supporters conceded faults.
**Q50.** (B) The lines mean that what is built with the greatest pride is often the most fragile and easily brought down.
