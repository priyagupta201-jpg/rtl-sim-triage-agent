### Passage 1 (Q21–Q25)

Read the passage and answer the questions that follow.

> When most people think of coral reefs, they imagine colourful underwater gardens full of darting fish. Few realise that a coral reef is not a plant at all, but a vast city built by millions of tiny animals called polyps. Each polyp is smaller than a grain of rice, yet together, over thousands of years, they construct limestone structures so large that some can be seen from space.
>
> The secret of the reef lies in a remarkable partnership. Inside the tissue of each coral polyp live microscopic algae. The algae use sunlight to make food, sharing much of it with the polyp; in return, the polyp gives the algae a safe home and the nutrients they need. This exchange is so efficient that reefs flourish even in the clear, nutrient-poor waters of tropical seas, where little else can grow.
>
> Yet this delicate balance is easily disturbed. When the water becomes too warm, the stressed polyps expel their algae and turn a ghostly white — a process known as "coral bleaching." Without their algae, the corals lose their main source of food and may soon starve. Scientists warn that if ocean temperatures continue to rise, many of the world's reefs could vanish within a single human lifetime, taking with them a quarter of all marine species that depend on them for shelter.

## Detailed Solutions

**Q1.** "Conscience" is the correct spelling.
**Q2.** "Rhythm" is the correct spelling.
**Q3.** The fixed collocation is "pay attention (to)."
**Q4.** One "sets" a personal best / a record.
**Q5.** "Put off" means to postpone.
**Q6.** "Get over" means to recover from something upsetting.
**Q7.** To "come clean" is to confess the truth.
**Q8.** The proverb warns against relying on something before it actually happens.
**Q9.** A "pedestrian" travels on foot.
**Q10.** A "eulogy" praises someone, often at their death.
**Q11.** "Abundant" means plentiful.
**Q12.** The opposite of "reluctant" (unwilling) is "eager."
**Q13.** The earlier past action needs the past perfect: "had left."
**Q14.** "Honest" begins with a silent 'h' (vowel sound), so "an."
**Q15.** "Famous for" is the correct preposition.
**Q16.** With "neither…nor," the verb agrees with the nearer subject "players," so "were."
**Q17.** "Must" expresses obligation (it is the law).
**Q18.** Positive statement with "has" takes the negative tag "hasn't she."
**Q19.** The question mark stays inside the quotation, and "Mother" is capitalised as a name.
**Q20.** "Effect" (noun) — a profound effect on medicine.
**Q21.** The passage states reefs are built by millions of polyps.
**Q22.** The algae share the food they make from sunlight with the polyp.
**Q23.** The efficient polyp–algae exchange lets reefs thrive in nutrient-poor water.
**Q24.** Bleaching occurs when warm water makes polyps expel their algae.
**Q25.** "Expel" here means to drive out.
**Q26.** The notice invites members to the reopening of the Children's Reading Wing.
**Q27.** A free entry pass must be collected before 10:15 a.m.
**Q28.** The workshop is for ages 8–12.
**Q29.** Photography is allowed only in the lobby.
**Q30.** The notice never mentions any cost for the workshop; the rest are stated.
**Q31.** Reassuring the nervous friend is the appropriate, supportive reply.
**Q32.** A polite decline of further items fits the waiter's offer.
**Q33.** Accepting congratulations graciously is appropriate.
**Q34.** A polite, honest apology answers the teacher's question.
**Q35.** "Excuse me, may I ask…" is the polite way to interrupt.

**Q37.** Politely alerting the stranger is the courteous response.
