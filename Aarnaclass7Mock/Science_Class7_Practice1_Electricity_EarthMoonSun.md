## Detailed Solutions

**Q1. (B)** A cell converts stored **chemical energy** into electrical energy to drive current through a circuit.

**Q2. (A)** In a battery the cells are joined **in series** — the positive terminal of one cell to the negative terminal of the next — so their voltages add up.

**Q3. (A)** A cell is shown by two parallel lines: a **long thin line (positive terminal)** and a **short thick line (negative terminal)**.

**Q4. (B)** An **open switch** is shown as a break in the wire with the lever lifted so the contact is broken; no current flows.

**Q5. (A)** A bulb is drawn as a **circle with a cross inside it**.

**Q6. (C)** Current flows only in a **complete (closed)** path from one terminal of the cell to the other.

**Q7. (B)** Current heating a wire is the **heating effect** of electric current.

**Q8. (B)** Heat produced depends on the wire's **material, length and thickness** (i.e. its resistance) and the current through it.

**Q9. (C)** For the same size, a wire of **higher resistance** produces **more heat**.

**Q10. (C)** **Nichrome** (an alloy of nickel and chromium) is used for heating coils.

**Q11. (B)** Nichrome has **high resistance** (so it gets very hot) and a **high melting point and resists oxidation**, so it does not melt or burn out quickly.

**Q12. (D)** An **electric bell** works on the magnetic effect, not the heating effect. Iron, heater and geyser all use the heating effect.

**Q13. (B)** The **filament** (usually tungsten) glows white-hot and gives light.

**Q14. (B)** A fuse **melts and breaks the circuit** when the current becomes dangerously large, protecting the appliance and wiring.

**Q15. (A)** A fuse is connected **in series** so that the whole current passes through it; then if the current is too high, the fuse melts and cuts off the entire circuit.

**Q16. (B)** When the live and neutral wires touch directly, a huge current flows — this is a **short circuit**.

**Q17. (A)** An **MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker)** switches off automatically on overload and can simply be switched back ON — no wire to replace.

**Q18. (C)** The deflection of a compass needle near a current-carrying wire shows the **magnetic effect** of current.

**Q19. (B)** A coil wound on an iron core that becomes magnetic only when current flows is an **electromagnet**.

**Q20. (B)** An electromagnet is made stronger by **increasing the number of turns of the coil and increasing the current** (and using a soft-iron core).

**Q21. (C)** An electric bell uses the **magnetic effect** — its electromagnet repeatedly pulls the hammer to strike the gong.

**Q22. (B)** The Earth spinning on its own axis is **rotation**.

**Q23. (B)** **Rotation** brings different parts of the Earth to face the Sun, causing day and night.

**Q24. (B)** The Moon has no light of its own; it **reflects sunlight**.

**Q25. (B)** In a **solar eclipse**, the **Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth**, blocking sunlight and casting its shadow on Earth.

**Q26. False.** The Moon does not produce its own light; it only reflects sunlight.

**Q27. One year.** One full revolution of the Earth around the Sun takes about one year (365¼ days). (One rotation takes about one day.)

**Q28. False.** A fuse is connected in **series**, not in parallel, so all the current passes through it.

**Q29. Phases.** The changing bright shapes (new moon, crescent, half, gibbous, full moon) are the **phases** of the Moon.

**Q30. True.** The Sun is a star and is the Earth's main source of light and heat.

**Q31. Lunar.** In a **lunar** eclipse the Earth is between the Sun and Moon, and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

**Q32. False.** Copper is a **very good conductor with low resistance**, so it is used for connecting wires, **not** for heating elements. Heating elements need high-resistance nichrome.

**Q33.** 1–c (heater → heating effect), 2–a (bell → magnetic effect), 3–b (rotation → spin on axis), 4–d (new moon → dark side faces Earth, Moon not visible).

**Q34.** Nichrome is used because **(i)** it has **high resistance**, so it produces a lot of heat when current flows, and **(ii)** it has a **high melting point and does not oxidise (burn out) easily** at high temperature, so the coil lasts long.

**Q35.** A fuse is connected **in series** so that the **entire current of the circuit passes through it**. When the current becomes too large (overloading or a short circuit), the fuse wire **heats up and melts, breaking the circuit** and stopping the current, which protects the appliances and prevents fire.

**Q36.** An electromagnet can be made stronger by: **(i)** increasing the **number of turns** in the coil, **(ii)** increasing the **current** flowing through it (e.g. using a stronger battery), and **(iii)** using a **soft-iron core** inside the coil.

**Q37.** In the circuit, the cell, switch and bulb are joined one after another by wires to form a single loop (series). **(a) Switch open:** the circuit is broken, no current flows, and the bulb does **not** glow. **(b) Switch closed:** the circuit is complete, current flows from the cell through the switch and bulb, and the bulb **glows**. *(Diagram: cell → wire → switch → wire → bulb → wire back to cell.)*

**Q38.** A **lunar eclipse** happens at full moon when the **Sun, Earth and Moon are in a straight line with the Earth in the middle** (Sun — Earth — Moon). The Earth blocks the sunlight and its **shadow falls on the Moon**, so the Moon looks dark for a while.

**Q39.** We always see only the **half of the Moon lit by the Sun**. As the Moon **revolves around the Earth**, the amount of its sunlit side that faces us keeps changing — from new moon (no bright part visible) to crescent, half, gibbous and full moon and back — giving the different **phases** over about a month.

**Q40.** **Rotation** is the spinning of the Earth on its own axis (about one day) and causes **day and night**. **Revolution** is the Earth's movement in its orbit around the Sun (about one year) and causes the **change of seasons / the year**.
