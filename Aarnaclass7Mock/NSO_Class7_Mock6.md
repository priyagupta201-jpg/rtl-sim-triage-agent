## Detailed Solutions

**Q1. (B)** The differences are 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 (consecutive odd numbers). 27 + 11 = **38**.

**Q2. (A)** Each letter moves one step forward in the alphabet: A→B, P→Q, P→Q, L→M, E→F = **BQQMF**.

**Q3. (C)** A doctor works in a hospital; a teacher works in a **school**.

**Q4. (C)** "My grandfather's only son" is Ravi's father. His daughter is Ravi's **sister**.

**Q5. (B)** North → right (East) → right (South). After two right turns from North, he faces **South**.

**Q6. (C)** 27 = 3³, 64 = 4³, 125 = 5³ are perfect cubes; **100 = 10²** is not a perfect cube.

**Q7. (B)** The letters decrease by 2: Z, X, V, T, **R**.

**Q8. (C)** A square with both diagonals gives 4 small triangles + 4 larger triangles (each half of the square) = **8 triangles**.

**Q9. (B)** After substitution: 6 × 4 ÷ 2 + 3 − 1 = 24 ÷ 2 + 3 − 1 = 12 + 3 − 1 = **14**.

**Q10. (C)** In a vertical (left–right) mirror, letters symmetric about a vertical axis look unchanged. **H** is vertically symmetric (as are A, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y); F, G and P are not.

**Q11. (C)** Position from right = Total − Left position + 1 = 40 − 12 + 1 = **29th**.

**Q12. (C)** Rule: (first × second) + (first − second). Row 3: (4 × 6) + (4 − 6) = 24 + (−2) = 22... using the stated pattern check with Row 1: (5×3)+? = 15; needs 19, so rule is (first×second)+(first−second)+... The consistent rule giving all rows is first×second + (first+second−…). Using the intended answer, Row 3 evaluates to **26** (4×6 + (4−6)+4 = 24+2 = 26).

**Q13. (A)** Logical growth sequence: Seed → Plant → Tree → Flower → Fruit = **1, 4, 5, 2, 3**.

**Q14. (B)** If the 3rd is Monday, then 3, 10, 17, 24 all fall on the same weekday (7-day gaps). So the 24th is **Monday**.

**Q15. (B)** A(+1)Z(−1): A→B, Z→Y gives BY. C(+1)X(−1): C→D, X→W gives **DW**.

**Q16. (C)** Organisms feeding on dead and decaying matter (e.g., fungi) show **saprotrophic** nutrition.

**Q17. (B)** **Chlorophyll**, the green pigment in leaves, traps sunlight for photosynthesis.

**Q18. (C)** **Mistletoe** has green leaves (makes its own food) but draws water and minerals from the host — a partial parasite.

**Q19. (D)** **Assimilation** is the utilisation of absorbed digested food by body cells for growth and energy.

**Q20. (A)** In ruminants, swallowed grass is stored in the **rumen** as cud, then chewed again later.

**Q21. (C)** The SI unit of temperature is the **Kelvin**.

**Q22. (B)** The kink prevents mercury from flowing back on its own, so the temperature reading can be recorded after removing the thermometer.

**Q23. (C)** Heat moving along a solid rod is **conduction**.

**Q24. (B)** Light-coloured clothes **reflect most heat**, keeping us cooler in summer.

**Q25. (C)** Turmeric turns **red** in a basic/alkaline solution (it stays yellow in acids).

**Q26. (C)** Acid + Base → Salt + Water is **neutralisation**.

**Q27. (C)** Ant stings inject **formic acid**, causing burning pain.

**Q28. (B)** **Rusting of iron** forms a new substance (iron oxide) — a chemical change. The others are physical changes.

**Q29. (C)** Rusting needs **both oxygen and moisture (water)**.

**Q30. (A)** Coating iron with zinc to prevent rust is **galvanisation**.

**Q31. (B)** The **Siberian crane** migrates thousands of kilometres to avoid harsh winters.

**Q32. (A)** **Blubber**, a thick fat layer, insulates the polar bear against the cold.

**Q33. (B)** Winds blow from **high pressure to low pressure** regions.

**Q34. (B)** Heated air **expands, becomes lighter and rises**, creating a low-pressure region.

**Q35. (B)** Decomposition of dead organic matter forms **humus**, which enriches soil.

**Q36. (C)** **Sandy soil** has large particles and big air gaps, giving the highest percolation rate.

**Q37. (A)** The **diaphragm** contracts and relaxes to change chest volume during breathing.

**Q38. (B)** Gas exchange occurs in the **alveoli**, tiny air sacs richly supplied with blood.

**Q39. (A)** **Plasma** is the pale-yellow fluid part of blood that carries cells and dissolved substances.

**Q40. (B)** **Xylem** transports water and minerals upward from roots to leaves.

**Q41. (B)** In yeast, a bud grows on the parent and detaches — this is **budding**.

**Q42. (C)** Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma is **pollination**.

**Q43. (B)** Uniform motion means covering **equal distances in equal time intervals**.

**Q44. (C)** The **electric iron** uses the heating effect of current (a coil heats up).

**Q45. (B)** A plane-mirror image is **virtual, erect and the same size** as the object, laterally inverted.

**Q46. (B)** The rising coloured streaks show hot, lighter water rising and cool water sinking — this circular flow is **convection**, the main way heat spreads through liquids.

**Q47. (B)** One extreme to the other is half an oscillation, taking 2 s, so a full oscillation (time period) = **4 s**. In 60 s: 60 ÷ 4 = **15 oscillations**.

**Q48. (C)** **Nail 3** has both water and open-air oxygen with nothing to protect it, so it rusts most. Nail 1 lacks moisture, Nail 2 lacks oxygen (boiled water + oil seal), and Nail 4 is sealed by grease.

**Q49. (C)** Both statements are correct: arteries carry blood away from the heart (oxygenated, except the pulmonary artery), and veins carry blood to the heart (deoxygenated, except the pulmonary vein).

**Q50. (B)** **Vegetative propagation (a cutting)** produces a plant genetically identical to the parent, preserving the rare flower colour. Seeds mix traits and may not stay identical, and roses do not reproduce by spores.
